# Level III and Higher Policy

## Decision for v1

No mandatory Level III is added to the initial 30-program blueprint.

This is deliberate, not a reduction in ambition.

One hundred substantial modules already provide:

- five undergraduate-scale subject blocks;
- five graduate/professional subject blocks;
- a complete canonical core;
- enough depth for professional readiness and future specialization.

Adding Modules 101–150 before the first 100 are authored, taught, reviewed, and used would encourage speculative filler.

## When Level III is justified

A Level III may be proposed only when all of the following hold:

1. Level I and II are complete and audited.
2. Real use reveals a coherent body of omitted expert material.
3. The material is not better owned by another program.
4. At least 50 distinct, non-filler modules can be defined.
5. The level has a clear audience and professional or research outcome.
6. It advances the field rather than rephrasing Level II.

## Likely forms

Level III should normally be a named specialization, for example:

- Programming Languages — dependent types and proof engineering;
- Database Engineering — optimizer research and storage-engine specialization;
- Security Engineering — cryptographic, hardware, offensive, or cloud specialization;
- Distributed Systems — Byzantine, geo-distributed, or formally verified systems;
- Computer Architecture — microarchitecture or accelerator specialization.

A specialization should not distort the universal 100-module core.
