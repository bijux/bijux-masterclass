# Worked answers — Orientation and the Platform Boundary

## 1. Why must Course 9 handoff bytes remain unchanged?

The platform may index and publish predecessor artifacts, but changing them would make Course 10 an unauthorized contract owner. Byte identity proves that platform metadata is derived from the released interface rather than silently rewriting it.

## 2. What is the difference between a platform integration and a platform guarantee?

An integration connects a tool such as a portal or gateway. A guarantee is an outcome such as immutable releases or accountable ownership. Integrations are replaceable; guarantees define the platform product and require evidence independent of one vendor.

## 3. Why choose one primary language track?

The learning objective is to master platform invariants, not duplicate framework syntax. One deep implementation plus a complete comparative track preserves cross-language reasoning without wasting the learner’s time.

## 4. What does the platform own?

It owns catalog ingestion, release publication, governance automation, lifecycle workflow, templates, discovery, sandboxing, artifact publication, runtime synchronization, and product evidence. Domain services still own business facts and decisions.

## 5. What is not proved by a dependency-light core?

It does not prove behavior of a hosted Backstage installation, cloud gateway, external registry, package registry, or hardened build platform. Those require environment-backed evidence.

## 6. Why are portal screenshots weak evidence?

A screenshot shows presentation at one moment. It does not prove authoritative ownership, release immutability, consumer safety, or that generated content matches source artifacts.
