# Canonical 30 Programs

The roster below is final for this blueprint. Names were normalized for grammar and professional usage without changing the user's intended domains.

| # | Program | Canonical purpose | Level-II professional outcome |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Programming Languages & Semantics** | Build a language-independent model of how programs are represented, typed, evaluated, composed, and given precise meaning. | Ready to reason across language families, review language designs, explain runtime and type-system trade-offs, and enter advanced language implementation or formal-semantics work. |
| 02 | **Programming Languages: Object-Oriented Programming** | Develop precise command of object models, object-oriented abstraction, polymorphism, composition, and maintainable object-oriented implementation across languages. | Ready to design, implement, test, review, and evolve substantial object-oriented systems in Java-, C#-, C++-, Python-, or similar ecosystems. |
| 03 | **Programming Languages: Functional Programming** | Develop practical and theoretical command of programming with immutable values, first-class functions, algebraic data, compositional effects, and equational reasoning. | Ready to build and review production functional or functional-first software in Haskell, Scala, F#, OCaml, Clojure, Rust, or functional Python ecosystems. |
| 04 | **Programming Languages: Concurrent & Parallel Programming** | Build precise, practical command of concurrency and parallelism across shared memory, asynchronous runtimes, actors, accelerators, and distributed-memory programming. | Ready to implement, debug, test, and optimize concurrent and parallel software using threads, async/await, actors, SIMD, OpenMP-style tasks, GPUs, and MPI-style message passing. |
| 05 | **Programming Languages: Systems Programming** | Develop command of low-level software construction where memory, representation, operating-system interfaces, latency, portability, and failure behavior are explicit. | Ready to build and maintain command-line tools, daemons, networking components, storage utilities, runtime components, and performance-sensitive libraries in C, C++, or Rust. |
| 06 | **Programming Languages: Metaprogramming** | Develop disciplined command of programs that inspect, generate, transform, or specialize programs while preserving readability, safety, tooling, and compatibility. | Ready to build and review macros, decorators, annotation processors, schema/code generators, typed derivation systems, build-time generators, and embedded DSLs. |
| 07 | **Programming Languages: Theory of Programming** | Build the mathematical foundations for specifying, proving, analyzing, and deriving programs, connecting logic, type theory, semantics, and static analysis. | Ready to read graduate programming-languages research, write formal specifications and proofs, use proof assistants and model checkers, and reason rigorously about program correctness. |
| 08 | **Compilers & Language Implementation** | Develop end-to-end command of interpreters, compilers, intermediate representations, analyses, optimization, code generation, runtimes, and production toolchains. | Ready to implement and maintain language front ends, transpilers, static analyzers, bytecode engines, optimizing compilers, DSL toolchains, and runtime components. |
| 09 | **Software Engineering: Software Design & Domain Modeling** | Develop the ability to turn ambiguous domain knowledge and changing requirements into precise, modular, testable software models. | Ready to lead code- and component-level design, facilitate domain discovery, define boundaries and contracts, and evolve models in business-critical software. |
| 10 | **Software Engineering: Methods & Evolution** | Develop disciplined methods for discovering, planning, building, maintaining, and changing software across its full economic and organizational lifecycle. | Ready to choose and adapt development methods, run effective Scrum or Kanban systems, manage requirements and risk, and lead the evolution of long-lived software. |
| 11 | **Software Engineering: Patterns** | Build a rigorous vocabulary for recurring software design problems, the forces that shape them, and the consequences of applying or misapplying established patterns. | Ready to recognize, select, adapt, combine, and critique design, integration, concurrency, data, and distributed-system patterns without pattern-driven overengineering. |
| 12 | **Software Engineering: Software Architecture** | Develop the ability to shape software-intensive systems through explicit quality attributes, boundaries, structures, deployment models, and evolutionary decisions. | Ready to act as a senior architect or technical lead who can design, document, evaluate, communicate, and evolve production architectures. |
| 13 | **Software Engineering: Testing & Quality Engineering** | Develop rigorous, economical approaches for evaluating software behavior, preventing defects, diagnosing failures, and making quality visible across the lifecycle. | Ready to design and lead testing strategies, build reliable automation, test distributed and data-intensive systems, and improve organizational quality engineering. |
| 14 | **Software Engineering: Software Delivery & Reliability Engineering** | Develop the engineering discipline required to turn source changes into safe production releases and keep services reliable under change, load, dependency failure, and operator error. | Ready to design CI/CD systems, release strategies, observability, SLOs, incident response, resilience mechanisms, and reliability programs for production services. |
| 15 | **Theory of Computation & Complexity** | Develop rigorous understanding of formal languages, computability, reductions, resource-bounded computation, and the structural limits of efficient algorithms. | Ready for graduate algorithms, cryptography, formal languages, complexity research, and principled reasoning about what software can and cannot compute efficiently. |
| 16 | **Algorithm Engineering** | Develop the full cycle of algorithmic work: precise problem formulation, design, proof, implementation, measurement, tuning, and selection under real machine and workload constraints. | Ready to solve unfamiliar computational problems, implement robust high-performance algorithms, compare alternatives empirically, and justify choices in production or research software. |
| 17 | **Data Structures Engineering** | Develop workload-aware command of in-memory, persistent, concurrent, external-memory, approximate, and distributed data structures, including their invariants and machine-level costs. | Ready to design, implement, test, benchmark, and select data structures for libraries, databases, runtimes, search systems, and high-performance applications. |
| 18 | **Database Engineering** | Develop end-to-end command of database storage, indexing, query processing, transactions, recovery, replication, distribution, specialization, and production operation. | Ready to design schemas and workloads, diagnose database behavior, tune and operate production databases, and contribute to database engines or data infrastructure. |
| 19 | **Data Engineering** | Develop command of reliable data ingestion, modeling, transformation, orchestration, quality, lineage, and delivery across batch and streaming workflows. | Ready to build, test, operate, and evolve production data pipelines and analytical data products using SQL, Python, databases, object storage, and modern orchestration systems. |
| 20 | **Data Architecture Engineering** | Develop the ability to shape organization-wide data structures, ownership, flows, platforms, governance, and evolution across operational, analytical, and AI use cases. | Ready to work as a data architect who can define target architectures, domain boundaries, integration strategies, governance mechanisms, migration plans, and decision records. |
| 21 | **Large-Scale Data Systems Engineering** | Develop deep command of distributed storage and computation mechanisms that make large-scale batch, streaming, and analytical systems correct, efficient, and operable. | Ready to build, tune, and diagnose cluster-scale data systems and to contribute to distributed processing, storage, query, or lakehouse infrastructure. |
| 22 | **Operating Systems** | Develop deep command of the abstractions, mechanisms, policies, and implementation trade-offs that mediate hardware resources and application execution. | Ready to work on kernels, runtimes, containers, low-level infrastructure, performance diagnosis, and operating-system-adjacent systems software. |
| 23 | **Computer Architecture** | Develop command of processor, memory, interconnect, accelerator, and system architecture from instruction-set contracts through microarchitectural performance and energy trade-offs. | Ready to reason about hardware-software performance, contribute to architecture or low-level systems work, and diagnose how software maps onto modern processors and memory systems. |
| 24 | **Network Engineering** | Develop protocol-level and operational command of how packets move through local, wide-area, wireless, datacenter, and cloud networks, including routing, transport, security, automation, and failure diagnosis. | Ready to design, configure, automate, troubleshoot, secure, and performance-engineer production networks and networked systems. |
| 25 | **Security Engineering** | Develop adversarial, risk-based command of designing, building, operating, and assessing trustworthy software and infrastructure. | Ready to perform threat modeling, design identity and access controls, secure applications and platforms, assess vulnerabilities, monitor attacks, and lead remediation and incident response. |
| 26 | **Systems Engineering** | Develop a disciplined, lifecycle-wide method for defining, modeling, integrating, verifying, operating, and evolving complex software-intensive and cyber-physical systems. | Ready to work as a systems engineer on complex products or platforms, managing stakeholder needs, requirements, interfaces, trade studies, risk, verification, and system evolution. |
| 27 | **Distributed Systems Engineering** | Develop rigorous command of computation across unreliable, concurrent machines, including time, ordering, replication, consistency, consensus, transactions, partitioning, and fault tolerance. | Ready to design, implement, test, and operate distributed services, coordination systems, replicated storage, event systems, and geo-distributed platforms. |
| 28 | **API & Service Engineering** | Develop end-to-end command of designing, implementing, securing, evolving, testing, and operating synchronous and asynchronous service interfaces. | Ready to build and lead production API and service work across HTTP/REST, RPC, GraphQL, events, gateways, contracts, security, reliability, and governance. |
| 29 | **Platform Engineering** | Develop the product, architecture, and implementation skills needed to create safe self-service platforms that reduce cognitive load and enable teams to deliver and operate software independently. | Ready to design, build, operate, and evolve internal developer platforms, paved roads, control planes, developer portals, and shared runtime capabilities. |
| 30 | **Cloud & Infrastructure Engineering** | Develop provider-independent command of compute, networking, storage, identity, automation, resilience, governance, and economics in public, private, hybrid, and multi-cloud infrastructure. | Ready to design, provision, secure, operate, migrate, and optimize production cloud infrastructure using infrastructure as code and modern managed services. |

## Normalized names

- `Programming Languages: Theory or programming` → **Programming Languages: Theory of Programming**
- `Software Engineering: patterns` → **Software Engineering: Patterns**
- `Data Architect Engineering` → **Data Architecture Engineering**
- `system engineering` → **Systems Engineering**
- `distributed system engineering` → **Distributed Systems Engineering**

These are naming corrections only. No requested pillar was removed or replaced.
