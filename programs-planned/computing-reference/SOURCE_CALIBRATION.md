# Source and Academic Calibration

Verified: 2026-08-28

The roadmaps are original curriculum designs. They are calibrated against current official bodies of knowledge and university course structures; they do not copy a university syllabus or claim academic equivalence.

## Core curriculum references

### ACM/IEEE-CS/AAAI CS2023

- Knowledge areas: <https://csed.acm.org/knowledge-areas/>
- Knowledge-model introduction: <https://csed.acm.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/1.3-Introduction-to-Knowledge-Model.pdf>
- Curriculum principles and cross-area connections: <https://csed.acm.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/2.1-Principles-and-Processes.pdf>

CS2023 explicitly separates algorithmic foundations, architecture, data management, foundations of programming languages, networking, operating systems, parallel and distributed computing, security, software development, and software engineering. It also stresses that a curriculum must connect these areas rather than teach them as islands.

### MIT EECS

- Computer Science and Engineering degree chart: <https://catalog.mit.edu/degree-charts/computer-science-engineering-course-6-3/>
- Current Course 6 subject descriptions: <https://catalog.mit.edu/subjects/6/>
- EECS subject groupings: <https://catalog.mit.edu/degree-charts/eecs-subject-groupings/>
- EECS tracks: <https://catalog.mit.edu/degree-charts/electrical-engineering-computer-science-tracks/>

The current MIT structure includes programming, software construction, mathematics for computer science, algorithms, computability and complexity, systems, operating systems or databases, low-level C and assembly, and computation structures. Advanced subjects include software design, language engineering, security, OS engineering, networking, multicore programming, distributed systems, databases, and architecture.

- MS overview: <https://www.cs.stanford.edu/masters-program-overview>

## Software and systems engineering references

### IEEE Computer Society SWEBOK V4

- <https://www.computer.org/education/bodies-of-knowledge/software-engineering>
- Topic map: <https://www.computer.org/education/bodies-of-knowledge/software-engineering/topics>

SWEBOK V4 is used to check coverage across requirements, design, construction, testing, maintenance, configuration, management, process, models and methods, quality, professional practice, economics, and computing foundations.

### SEBoK 2.14

- <https://sebokwiki.org/>
- <https://sebokwiki.org/wiki/SEBoK_Introduction>

SEBoK 2.14 was released in May 2026 and provides a living reference for systems thinking, lifecycle processes, MBSE, systems of systems, specialty engineering, and professional practice.

### INCOSE Systems Engineering Handbook, Fifth Edition

- <https://www.incose.org/resources-publications/technical-publications/se-handbook/>

The handbook is used to calibrate lifecycle processes, requirements, architecture, integration, verification, validation, risk, and technical management.

## Interpretation rule

University and body-of-knowledge references provide:

- scope checks;
- prerequisite checks;
- rigor calibration;
- missing-topic detection;
- terminology alignment.

They do not determine module count, wording, or sequence automatically.

Every roadmap still requires professor-level and practitioner-level judgment.
