# Supply-Chain Policy

## One-Page Review

### Governing question

How should a platform decide whether an exact artifact may be built, published, promoted, and executed by verifying its identity, origin, builder, inputs, signatures, attestations, components, vulnerabilities, exceptions, and revocation state at every material boundary?

### Essential definitions

- An **artifact** is immutable build output identified by a cryptographic digest: image, package, binary, archive, manifest, model, or configuration bundle.
- A **tag/version name** is a mutable discovery reference unless the repository contract makes it immutable; a **digest** identifies exact bytes under an algorithm.
- A **signature** binds a key or signing identity to a payload or artifact digest; it does not by itself establish that the code is safe or the build was trustworthy.
- An **attestation** is authenticated metadata containing a subject digest, predicate type, and predicate claims under a defined schema.
- **provenance** describes how an artifact was produced, including builder, build process, source/materials, invocation, and outputs at the supported assurance level.
- An **SBOM** inventories components and dependency relationships with schema, generation method, artifact binding, and completeness.
- **VEX** communicates a supplier’s analysis of whether a known vulnerability affects a product; it is scoped evidence, not a universal waiver.
- A **trust root** authorizes keys, certificate authorities, identity issuers, transparency logs, timestamp authorities, or verification metadata.
- **keyless signing** uses a short-lived certificate binding an ephemeral key to an authenticated identity; verification still constrains issuer, subject, artifact, and log/time evidence.
- A **transparency log** provides append-only, publicly auditable evidence that a signing event was recorded; inclusion is not approval.
- A **promotion** authorizes the same digest to enter a higher environment after required evidence and approvals, without rebuilding it.
- **quarantine** prevents further promotion/use while preserving artifact and evidence for analysis, correction, or controlled exception.

### Central model, mechanism, or theorem

Let artifact $a$ have digest $d=H(bytes(a))$ and evidence set $E_d$. A policy decision is

$$
Admit(a,c)=Digest(a,d)\land Signer(E_d,T)\land Provenance(E_d,B,S,M)
\land Inventory(E_d,Q)\land Risk(E_d,c)\land Current(T,R,X,c),
$$

where $T$ is current trust, $B$ allowed builders, $S$ allowed source/build definitions, $M$ expected materials, $Q$ evidence-quality/completeness requirements, $R$ revocations, $X$ bounded exceptions, and $c$ deployment context. Every claim is bound to the exact deployed digest. A valid signature cannot substitute for provenance, an SBOM, vulnerability policy, authorization, or current revocation checks.

### Critical invariants

- Deployment policy resolves references to and records an immutable manifest digest; later tag movement cannot change admitted bytes.
- Every signature, provenance statement, SBOM, scan, VEX, approval, and exception is matched to the exact subject digest and expected predicate/schema.
- Verification constrains signer identity and issuer/key, not merely cryptographic validity or presence of any signature.
- Build provenance is generated by the build platform, authentic, complete enough for the claimed level, and compared with expected source, workflow, builder, and parameters.
- Untrusted build steps cannot access the provenance-signing key or rewrite platform-generated provenance for high-assurance builds.
- SBOM completeness, generator, time, format, component identity, and artifact binding are evaluated before vulnerability/license policy consumes it.
- Vulnerability findings are time-sensitive; severity, exploitability, reachability, fix availability, environment, and exception expiry remain distinct.
- The same verified digest moves through environments; rebuilding for production creates a different subject requiring new evidence.
- Policy runs at publication/promotion and at authoritative admission/runtime pull boundaries, with background re-evaluation after new intelligence or revocation.
- Trust-root, issuer, builder, signer, scanner, repository, and policy changes are versioned, reviewed, rotated, and rapidly revocable.
- Exceptions are artifact- and context-specific, owned, approved, compensated, visible, and expiring; a tag-, repository-, or team-wide waiver is not acceptable by default.
- Verification outages have bounded cached-evidence behavior; unknown, stale, malformed, revoked, or unreachable evidence is never silently treated as verified.

### Common failures

Deploying tags; checking a digest without authenticating origin; accepting any valid signature; signer regex too broad; signing on a developer laptop with release authority; provenance self-asserted by build steps; trusting branch name without immutable source commit; rebuild per environment; SBOM not bound to artifact; scanner database age ignored; severity alone treated as exploitability; VEX accepted from an unauthorized issuer; attestation existence checked but predicate ignored; admission checks only top-level containers; registry mirror changes bytes; mutable policy/trust fetched without pinning; transparency-log presence called approval; key rotation leaves old compromised key valid; exception names a tag; policy-controller namespace not opted in; admission outage fails open; and rollback restores revoked trust.

### Compact example

Release workflow builds image digest `sha256:d1`, emits platform-signed SLSA provenance naming immutable source commit, workflow and isolated builder, produces a digest-bound SPDX SBOM, and records vulnerability results. Keyless signature certificate must have the approved CI issuer and immutable repository/workflow identity, with transparency/timestamp evidence. Promotion policy verifies all subjects equal `d1`, builder/source/materials match release expectations, SBOM completeness is acceptable, vulnerability data is fresh, and no signer/builder/digest is revoked. Production admission resolves the image to `d1`, repeats verification under current policy, and records decision provenance. A seven-day vulnerability exception applies only to `d1` and one service/environment. Production never rebuilds the image.

## Governing Question

How can a platform turn “run trusted software” into explicit, machine-verifiable predicates without equating trust with a registry location, signature presence, scanner result, or vendor name?

A supply chain is a graph of sources, dependencies, tools, build workers, identities, repositories, attestations, promotions, and deployment environments. Each edge can be compromised or misunderstood. Policy must state which evidence supports which claim and bind it to immutable artifacts.

The platform’s job is not to prove software harmless. It is to verify that production runs the exact artifact approved under a declared process and current risk posture, and to make exceptions and residual uncertainty visible.

## Why This Matters

Compromise before deployment can bypass runtime defenses. An attacker who changes source, a dependency, build worker, artifact, tag, provenance, registry, or signing authority may deliver code through the normal release path.

Signatures solve only authenticity under a trust decision. A malicious but authorized builder can sign harmful output. Provenance adds traceability and build-process claims; an SBOM adds inventory; scanning adds time-sensitive observations. None is interchangeable.

Artifacts continue to age after admission. New vulnerabilities, compromised keys, malicious packages, revoked builders, or incorrect attestations appear later. Continuous re-evaluation and response are part of policy, not optional hygiene.

Supply-chain controls can also halt delivery. Registry, transparency, identity, scanner, or admission outages create pressure to bypass. Cached evidence, local verification, last-known-good trust, quarantine, and emergency policy must be designed before incidents.

## Prerequisites and Retrieval

Retrieve cryptographic hashes and collision resistance; digital signatures, certificates, OIDC, timestamps, transparency logs, and trust roots; content-addressable storage; OCI images/indexes/manifests/descriptors/referrers; build systems and isolated runners; Git commits/tags; package dependency graphs; in-toto statements; SLSA tracks/levels and provenance; SPDX/CycloneDX SBOMs; vulnerability identifiers/severity/exploitability/VEX; artifact repositories; policy as code; Kubernetes images/admission; revocation; incident response; and evidence retention.

Distinguish name/tag from digest; integrity from authenticity; signer from builder; source author from approver; provenance from signature; attestation envelope from predicate; SBOM from vulnerability scan; component presence from reachability; severity from risk; VEX from exemption; build isolation from hermeticity/reproducibility; publication from promotion; admission-time from continuous policy; transparency from trust; freshness from timestamp; and rollback from re-authorizing revoked artifacts.

## Core Exposition

### Model an evidence graph, not a badge

Start with exact artifact digest. Attach authenticated statements: signature, provenance, SBOM, scan, VEX, test, license review, promotion approval, and exception. Each statement has issuer, schema/predicate type, subject digest, time, validity, and trust policy.

Evidence edges answer different questions. Signature: who controlled an authorized signing identity? Provenance: what builder/process/inputs produced the output? SBOM: what components are declared? Scan: what did a tool/database observe? Approval: who accepted a bounded risk?

Avoid one `trusted=true` label. Preserve claims and reasons so policy can change without rebuilding history. Store immutable evidence by digest and retain verification metadata.

Graph completeness is scoped. Missing source material, transitive dependency, platform layer, or predicate means the corresponding claim is unknown—not false and not automatically safe.

### Pin and verify artifact identity

An OCI descriptor digest is content address. The consumer recomputes digest over retrieved bytes and checks size before trusting content. An image index points to platform-specific manifests, so policy must know whether it admits the index, selected manifest, or both.

Tags are useful human discovery handles but can move. Resolve at promotion/admission and persist the selected digest. Prefer manifests that already specify digest so desired state is reviewable.

Digest integrity does not establish provenance. An attacker who controls the reference channel can provide a malicious artifact and its correct digest. Obtain expected digest through authenticated signature, provenance, promotion record, or trusted manifest.

Test registry mirrors, multi-architecture selection, garbage collection, copy, and restoration. Evidence discovery must follow the same subject digest after promotion between repositories.

### Verify authorized signer identity

Cryptographic verification establishes that a key signed a payload. Policy establishes whether that key or certificate identity was authorized for this artifact and context at signing time.

For keyless Sigstore-style verification, constrain certificate OIDC issuer and exact subject identity, verify certificate chain and signing payload, and validate transparency/timestamp evidence under configured trust. Shared multi-tenant issuers require repository/organization/workflow constraints.

For managed/KMS keys, protect key use with workload identity, least privilege, approval, logging, and rotation. Do not expose release signing keys to arbitrary build commands.

Support threshold or independent roles for high-risk release: builder attestation plus release approval, rather than several signatures controlled by one compromised identity.

### Validate attestation structure and semantics

An in-toto Statement binds subject resource descriptors to a predicate type and predicate. Verification must authenticate the envelope, match exact subject digest, require expected predicate type/schema, and evaluate predicate content.

“Attestation exists” is not a useful guarantee. An attacker may sign an empty or self-declared predicate. Require authorized issuer and semantic fields.

Handle multiple subjects, names, algorithms, media types, and unknown extensions explicitly. Prevent predicate-type confusion: an SBOM predicate cannot satisfy provenance merely because both are JSON.

Record verifier/parser versions. Schema evolution can change accepted fields, defaulting, or canonicalization; compatibility tests accompany upgrades.

### Evaluate provenance against expectations

Expected provenance includes allowed build platform identity, immutable source repository/commit, approved build definition/workflow, controlled parameters, declared materials, output subject, and required completeness.

SLSA organizes build assurance into levels. Provenance existence helps traceability but can be forged at low assurance. Higher levels add hosted and hardened build-platform controls, including separation of signing material from untrusted build steps.

Do not assign a level because a JSON document has a SLSA predicate URI. Validate every requirement for the applicable specification version and evidence from platform operations.

Separate source and build claims. Code review, protected references, and source history do not prove isolated build; hardened build does not prove source changes were appropriately approved.

### Protect the build platform

Use ephemeral isolated runners for release, pinned build definitions/actions/tools, least-privilege network and secrets, immutable source input, and workload identity. Separate untrusted pull-request builds from privileged release.

Platform generates provenance outside user-controlled build steps and signs under an identity the build cannot access. Include all relevant top-level inputs and builder identity.

Control dependency resolution: lockfiles, immutable registries, checksums, vendor caches, and policy for network-fetched inputs. Undeclared material weakens reproduction and incident analysis.

Build logs are not provenance and may leak secrets. Retain safe reproducibility/debug evidence without treating mutable logs as signed claims.

### Generate and qualify SBOMs

Generate SBOM from exact artifact/build context, not only source manifests. Include direct and transitive components, versions, package identifiers, hashes, relationships, supplier/license fields as needed, and state completeness.

SPDX and CycloneDX provide interoperable models; choose supported versions and validate schema. Consumers must understand which ecosystems, layers, generated/vendored/static dependencies, and build/runtime components the generator covers.

Bind SBOM to artifact digest in an authenticated attestation. An unsigned filename next to an image tag can be replaced or mismatched.

Track generator/version, generation time, completeness, errors, and merge method. Combining base-image and application SBOMs requires correct identities and dependency relationships, not concatenation alone.

### Turn vulnerability data into risk policy

A vulnerability scanner joins inventory/content with a database and heuristics at time $t$. Results depend on identifiers, package metadata, database freshness, and scanner behavior.

Policy can consider severity, known exploitation, reachability, environment exposure, privilege, data sensitivity, fix availability, component age, and compensating controls. A universal “no critical CVEs” threshold is simple but often fails on false positives, unpatched bases, or irrelevant components.

VEX can state not-affected/affected/fixed/under-investigation with rationale. Accept only authorized, digest/product-scoped, current VEX and retain counter-evidence. It is not the same as an organization approving deployment risk.

Rescan/re-evaluate existing digests when databases or policy change. Avoid requiring rebuild solely to refresh scan metadata when the artifact bytes are unchanged; attach new time-bounded evidence.

### Separate build, publish, promote, and run gates

Build gate validates source/workflow inputs and produces evidence. Publication gate prevents uncontrolled artifacts entering trusted repositories. Promotion gate authorizes one digest for an environment. Admission gate ensures deployed digest and current evidence satisfy policy.

CI checks are early feedback, not authoritative if direct registry or cluster paths exist. Registry permissions, promotion service, admission policy, and runtime pull controls must align.

Promote the same digest. Rebuilding per environment creates new bytes/provenance and breaks the tested-to-production identity chain. Environment-specific configuration belongs outside artifact where possible.

Record who/what promoted, from/to, digest, policy/evidence revisions, exceptions, and time. Promotion does not make future revocation irrelevant.

### Enforce at Kubernetes admission safely

Inspect every image field in Pods and workload templates, including init and ephemeral containers. Resolve tags to digests without time-of-check/time-of-use gaps and store the digest in admitted state when mechanism supports it.

Sigstore policy-controller and Kyverno image verification can validate signatures/attestations and mutate tag references to digests. Their matching, namespace opt-in, no-match behavior, trust roots, availability, caching, and versions are part of the security boundary.

Admission may need registry/evidence/network access. Use local caches and bounded last-known-good trust, high availability, short timeouts, staged warn/enforce rollout, and fail behavior matching risk.

Validate final state after mutation. A signature-checking mutator that fails open needs a final authoritative validator; admission still does not cover direct node/runtime execution.

### Govern trust roots and verification policy

Inventory signing keys, Fulcio-like CAs, OIDC issuers, transparency logs, timestamp authorities, TUF/update roots, builder identities, scanners, repositories, predicate schemas, and policy bundles.

Bootstrap roots out of band and pin versions/digests. Rotate with overlap no longer than required to verify legitimate historical signing times. Distinguish “trusted to verify historical signature” from “authorized to sign new release.”

Revocation may target key, certificate identity pattern, issuer, builder, repository, workflow, artifact digest, package, or evidence issuer. Distribute emergency denial faster than normal policy when necessary.

Test trust compromise and restore. Backup recovery must not resurrect revoked keys, builders, exceptions, or artifacts.

### Design exceptions and quarantine

An exception names exact artifact digest, failed policy IDs/reasons, service/tenant/environment, owner, independent approver, risk, compensating controls, issue, creation, expiry, and maximum renewal.

Never exempt a mutable tag: it can point to different bytes during the waiver. Repository/team-wide exceptions are rarely bounded enough.

Quarantine blocks new promotion/deployment while preserving artifact and evidence. Decide whether running instances stop immediately, drain, or continue based on exploit and availability risk.

Emergency authorization produces visible decision evidence and triggers follow-up. Expiry automatically denies; deleting a ticket does not remove distributed policy unless reconciliation does.

### Continuously re-evaluate and respond

Maintain deployment inventory mapping digest to workloads, tenants, environments, nodes, owners, evidence, and decisions. New vulnerability or revocation events query this inventory.

Responses include alert, block new deployment, freeze promotion, revoke trust, quarantine, roll back/forward to safe digest, rotate credentials, isolate runtime, or emergency remove. Define authority and SLA by risk.

Rollback candidate must itself satisfy current policy. “Previously deployed” is not evidence after a signer or artifact is revoked.

Measure time from intelligence/revocation to effective denial and remediation across registry, promotion, admission, caches, and running workload.

### Preserve evidence and privacy

Record artifact digest, signer/builder identity, issuer, source commit/workflow, predicate types and digests, SBOM/scan revision/freshness, policy version, exception, decision reason, verifier version, and enforcement outcome.

Do not log signing tokens, private keys, registry credentials, full environment/source secrets, or unnecessary personal identity. Use immutable IDs and controlled access to evidence.

Transparency logs expose signing identities/events by design; choose identities and disclosure appropriately. Internal logs require retention, integrity, time synchronization, and deletion policy.

Historical reproduction needs the exact artifact, evidence, trust/policy snapshot, parser/verifier version, and revocation knowledge applicable to the question. Current verification and historical-at-time verification are different queries.

### Migrate incrementally

Inventory registries, tags, build systems, signers/keys, artifacts, deployment paths, SBOM/scanners, direct cluster users, and exceptions. Identify authoritative production pull paths.

First pin digests and generate visibility evidence. Introduce workload-identity signing and platform provenance, then enforce signer/builder/source predicates. Add qualified SBOM and risk policy after measuring coverage and freshness.

Run warn/audit and backtest current digests. Remediate or create narrow expiring exceptions. Canary enforcement by service/namespace and ensure break-glass is accountable.

Remove old signing keys, tag-only paths, direct publish permissions, unsigned caches, and fallback admission bypass. Prove restore cannot re-enable them.

## Definitions and Notation

Let artifacts be $A$, digests $D$, evidence $E$, trust policy $T_v$, risk intelligence $R_t$, and deployment contexts $C$.

Artifact identity is

$$
d=alg:Encode(H_{alg}(bytes(a))).
$$

An attestation statement is

$$
e=(type,subjects,predicateType,predicate,issuer,signature,time),
$$

and $Bound(e,d)$ holds only when an allowed subject descriptor contains the expected algorithm/digest for $d$ under the predicate contract.

Verification at time $t$ is

$$
V(a,c,t)=Digest(a,d)\land Auth(E_d,T_v,t)\land Claims(E_d,c)
\land Fresh(E_d,R_t,c)\land \neg Revoked(d,E_d,T_v,t)\land ExceptionOK(d,c,t).
$$

Let discovery/publish, promotion, admission, and runtime inventory surfaces be $S$. Revocation convergence for digest $d$ issued at $t_r$ is

$$
L_{revoke}(d)=\max_{s\in S}(t_s-t_r),
$$

where $t_s$ is the time surface $s$ prevents the prohibited new effect or applies its documented running-workload response.

## Mechanisms, Invariants, or Proof Structure

### Supply-chain policy mechanism

1. Identify exact artifact types, registries, environments, owners, build/publish/promotion/run paths, and bypasses.
2. Define current trust roots and allowed signer, issuer, builder, source, workflow, materials, predicate/schema, and risk requirements.
3. Build in isolated governed infrastructure from immutable source/definition; platform generates and authenticates provenance.
4. Compute artifact digest, publish immutable bytes, signature, provenance, qualified SBOM, scans, tests, and other evidence bound to it.
5. Validate envelope/signature, exact subjects and predicate types, semantic claims, completeness, freshness, and revocation.
6. Publish/promote the same digest under policy, approvals, separation of duties, and narrow expiring exceptions.
7. Resolve deployment references to digest and enforce current policy at authoritative admission/pull paths for every artifact field.
8. Record verification and enforcement provenance without secrets; maintain digest-to-deployment inventory.
9. Re-evaluate on new vulnerabilities, VEX, trust/policy/data changes, compromise, exception expiry, and artifact discovery.
10. Quarantine, deny, rotate, isolate, or replace under risk-specific response and measured convergence.
11. Test outages, stale caches, tag races, registry copies, multi-platform indexes, parser upgrades, revocation, rollback, and restore.
12. Retire old keys/builders/repositories/policies/evidence paths and prove fallback or recovery cannot resurrect them.

### Artifact invariant

Every policy claim and deployment refers to the same verified immutable digest; a mutable name never transfers approval or exception to new bytes.

### Evidence invariant

Every accepted statement has authenticated authorized issuer, exact subject binding, expected predicate/schema, semantically validated claims, and sufficient completeness/freshness for its use.

### Promotion invariant

The digest tested and approved is the digest promoted and executed. Environment transition changes authorization/configuration, not artifact bytes.

### Trust invariant

Authorization of signers, builders, evidence issuers, roots, and artifacts is current and revocable; historical cryptographic validity alone cannot authorize new execution.

### Exception invariant

Every bypass is digest-, policy-, service-, tenant-, and environment-scoped, independently approved, visible, compensated, and automatically invalid after expiry.

### Proof sketch: tag-scoped approval cannot establish artifact approval

Suppose policy approves tag `app:stable` at time $t_1$ when it resolves to safe digest $d_1$. Tags are mutable, so at $t_2$ an authorized or compromised publisher changes it to malicious digest $d_2\ne d_1$. A deployment checking only the approved tag sees the same name and admits different bytes.

Therefore equality of tag names cannot prove equality of reviewed artifacts. Binding approval, evidence, exception, and admitted state to $d_1$ prevents transfer to $d_2$; the new digest requires its own verification. This argument assumes a collision-resistant digest and correct byte verification, and does not claim that digest alone proves provenance or safety.

### Verification strategy

Test digest recomputation, manifest/index selection, tag movement, registry copy/mirror, subject mismatch, multiple subjects, predicate confusion, unknown algorithm/schema, signer/issuer patterns, timestamps, transparency, and revoked trust.

Validate provenance against allowed builder/source/workflow/material/parameter expectations and the claimed SLSA specification requirements. Attempt user-controlled provenance and signing-key access.

Test SBOM schema, generator, completeness, digest binding, transitive relationships, base images, scan freshness, identifier ambiguity, VEX authority, exception expiry, and changed vulnerability intelligence.

Exercise publish/promotion/admission/runtime bypass, every image field, policy/evidence/registry outage, partial cache, rollback, emergency denial, key rotation/compromise, quarantine, and disaster restore.

## Alternatives and Trade-Offs

- **Tag versus digest.** Tags aid discovery and release channels; digests bind exact bytes. Use both, but authorize the digest.
- **Long-lived key versus keyless identity.** Managed keys work offline/legacy; keyless reduces key custody while adding issuer/log/time trust.
- **One signer versus threshold roles.** One is simple; independent build and release authorities reduce single compromise at operational cost.
- **Self-hosted versus hosted hardened build.** Self-hosting increases control and maintenance burden; hosted platforms may provide stronger isolation evidence but add supplier trust.
- **Reproducible versus authenticated build.** Reproducibility enables comparison; authenticated isolated provenance establishes process identity. They complement each other.
- **SPDX versus CycloneDX.** Both support supply-chain inventory with different ecosystems/models; schema and consumer capability matter more than label.
- **Source versus artifact SBOM.** Source inventory is early; artifact inventory better reflects shipped bytes. Generate/merge with explicit completeness.
- **Severity threshold versus contextual risk.** Thresholds are clear; context reduces noise but needs reliable exploitability/reachability evidence.
- **Online scan versus signed snapshot.** Online is current but adds latency/outage; snapshots are reproducible but age. Enforce bounded freshness.
- **Admission versus registry promotion.** Promotion blocks distribution early; admission rechecks current context/trust. Use both for defense in depth.
- **Resolve tag by mutation versus require submitted digest.** Resolution improves usability; explicit digest improves reviewability and avoids registry-time ambiguity.
- **Public versus private transparency.** Public logs improve ecosystem audit; private systems protect metadata but require independent governance.
- **Central versus per-team trust policy.** Central baselines prevent weak islands; team overlays support domain constraints without loosening mandatory rules.
- **Fail closed versus cached verification.** Fail closed preserves currency but can halt delivery; bounded verified cache improves availability without accepting unknown evidence.
- **Stop versus continue running revoked artifact.** Immediate stop limits exploit; controlled drain may preserve critical safety/availability. Decide by response tier.
- **Rebuild versus re-attest.** Code/dependency changes require rebuild; new scan/risk observations can attest unchanged digest.
- **Broad versus digest-specific exception.** Broad is convenient and transferable; digest-specific makes risk bounded and reviewable.
- **Rollback versus forward fix.** Rollback is fast only if old digest remains trusted; forward fix avoids reintroducing known vulnerability.

## Failure Modes and Misconceptions

- “The registry is trusted, so every image is trusted” confuses storage with artifact authority.
- “The tag is immutable enough” lacks an enforced contract and digest evidence.
- “Digest proves who built it” proves byte identity only.
- “Any valid signature is acceptable” omits signer authorization.
- “Transparency-log inclusion means approved” records an event, not policy permission.
- “Keyless means trustless” still trusts issuer, CA, log/time material, and identity mapping.
- “Developer signature equals release provenance” conflates roles and build isolation.
- “A SLSA predicate URI proves a SLSA level” requirements need evidence.
- “Build steps may generate their own high-assurance provenance” permits self-assertion.
- “Source commit proves dependencies” ignores fetched/generated/material inputs.
- “An SBOM is complete because it parses” completeness and generator coverage are separate.
- “SBOM proves absence” incomplete inventory cannot.
- “No critical CVEs means safe” ignores unknown flaws and other risks.
- “Critical CVE always means exploitable here” ignores context while still requiring justified analysis.
- “VEX is an exception” supplier analysis and deployment risk acceptance differ.
- “Old scan remains current for immutable bytes” intelligence changes.
- “Production rebuild is equivalent” creates a new digest and evidence subject.
- “CI verification protects direct deployment” admission/pull paths may bypass it.
- “Top-level container scan covers all images” misses init, ephemeral, and injected containers.
- “Signature policy automatically resolves tag race” mechanism/configuration must bind persisted digest.
- “Cached trust can live forever during outage” turns stale authority into permanent access.
- “Rotating key revokes old authorization” old key may still verify/sign unless policy changes.
- “Previously deployed is safe to roll back” current revocation/risk may forbid it.
- “Exception on a tag is narrow” tag movement transfers it.
- “Delete the malicious image to finish response” running/cached copies and evidence remain.
- “Backup restore should restore every trust object” may resurrect revoked authorities.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

`app:stable` points to digest `d1` during review and `d2` at deployment. Tag-only policy admits `d2`; digest-bound desired state rejects mismatch and requires evidence for `d2`.

### Example 2

Image has valid signature from an employee test key. Verification succeeds cryptographically but policy rejects because production requires CI issuer and exact immutable release-workflow subject.

### Example 3

Build job emits provenance itself and signs with secret available to build script. It cannot support hardened provenance because compromised build commands can forge both output and claims. Platform-side isolated signing repairs the boundary.

### Example 4

Multi-architecture index is signed, but selected ARM manifest has no required SBOM. Policy explicitly requires index authorization plus evidence bound to selected platform manifest before scheduling.

### Example 5

SBOM lists direct packages but marks transitive dependencies unknown. Schema validation passes; completeness policy denies production until generator/merge covers the required ecosystem.

### Example 6

Critical CVE appears in a library whose vulnerable function is unreachable. Authorized VEX records analysis for exact product/digest; local risk owner approves seven-day exception with monitoring and upgrade deadline. Neither artifact nor VEX alone grants deployment.

### Example 7

Scanner database becomes stale during outage. Previously verified low-risk digests may deploy within a four-hour cache window; new or high-risk production artifacts fail closed. Alerts expose shrinking freshness budget.

### Example 8

Release digest is copied from staging registry to production without rebuild. OCI digest and evidence subjects remain identical; promotion record adds environment authorization and admission verifies from production location.

### Example 9

CI signer identity is compromised. Emergency policy revokes issuer-subject/workflow after a cutoff, blocks new promotion/admission, inventories running digests, and separates older verified releases from suspicious window for response.

### Example 10

Rollback requests yesterday’s digest, but current intelligence marks it malicious. Promotion rejects “previously approved”; team deploys a new safe digest with complete evidence instead.

## Cross-Program Connections

- **Software Supply Chain Security:** provenance, attestations, SBOMs, signing, transparency, dependencies, and build isolation are the core evidence model.
- **Cryptography:** hashes, signatures, certificates, timestamps, logs, roots, and revocation make artifact claims verifiable.
- **DevOps and Delivery Engineering:** protected source, ephemeral runners, immutable builds, promotion, GitOps, and release roles create evidence.
- **Kubernetes Engineering:** OCI references, admission, image pull, container variants, runtime caches, and node bypass determine enforcement.
- **Security Engineering:** threat models, least privilege, separation of duties, vulnerability response, exceptions, and incident containment govern trust.
- **Data Engineering:** evidence schemas, lineage graphs, completeness, freshness, joins, retention, and reprocessing support decisions.
- **API and Service Engineering:** artifact/evidence registries, immutable identifiers, schemas, pagination, errors, and consistency expose the control plane.
- **Distributed Systems:** registries, mirrors, caches, tag races, partial outage, replication, and convergence shape correctness.
- **Reliability Engineering:** verification latency, cached evidence, quarantine, rollout/rollback, and critical-service response balance availability.
- **Governance and Compliance:** approvals, control mapping, licenses, attestations, audit retention, supplier assertions, and exceptions make risk accountable.

## Answers to the Exercises

### Answer 1

Require exact OCI index/manifest digest and byte verification; approved registry is location only. Verify signature under pinned CA/key/TUF roots, exact OIDC issuer and immutable repository/release-workflow subject, transparency/timestamp evidence, and signing-time authorization. Require platform-generated authenticated SLSA provenance with allowed builder, immutable source commit, reviewed workflow, parameters/materials, and claimed-level evidence.

Require authenticated digest-bound SPDX/CycloneDX SBOM with approved generator/version, ecosystems/layers, dependency relationships, completeness, and generation time. Risk decision uses current scanner/database, severity, exploitability, reachability, fix and environment. Accept VEX only from authorized supplier for exact product/digest/status/rationale and require separate local risk approval when policy fails.

Promote same digest with decision/policy/evidence revisions and separation of duties. Admission rechecks every image field under current revocation. Define evidence ages by type, trust-root rotation/revocation, bounded outage cache, and digest/service/environment-specific exception with owner, approver, controls, and expiry.

### Answer 2

Threats include untrusted fork obtaining release token; reusable repository name; broad subject regex; workflow/action replacement; hosted-runner cross-tenant leakage; build step accessing signing material; OIDC/CA/log/TUF compromise; tag movement; registry overwrite/mirror substitution; forged predicate; and admission fail-open.

Protect environment with branch/ref and independent approval; require immutable organization/repository/workflow identifiers and exact audience/issuer; pin actions/build definition; isolate ephemeral hosted builders; generate provenance/sign outside untrusted step; verify certificate chain, identity, transparency inclusion/timestamp, subject digest, and predicate semantics under pinned roots.

Use digest-only promotion, immutable repository, least-privilege publish, HA admission with local verified cache and current revocation. Monitor issuance/signing/publish/promotion/admission identities and digests. Drill cutoff revocation, root/log outage, suspicious-window inventory, and restore without revoked trust.

### Answer 3

Identify OCI index plus each selected platform manifest. Generate SBOM for shipped filesystem/config/build outputs per manifest and an index-level mapping. Merge base-image SBOM by exact base digest; scan application package managers, vendored source/binaries, generated assets, and static-link symbols/build metadata with explicit method.

Validate SPDX/CycloneDX schema and package identifiers/hashes/relationships. Record generator, version, time, errors, unsupported ecosystems, and composition completeness. Bind each SBOM attestation to correct manifest digest; index signature alone cannot transfer ARM SBOM to AMD64.

Evaluate vulnerability database freshness, identifier mapping, severity, exploitation, reachability, environment, and fixes. Unknown/incomplete inventory is a policy state. Authorized digest-scoped VEX supplies analysis but local exception handles accepted residual risk. Re-evaluate unchanged digests as intelligence changes.

### Answer 4

Disable signer authorization and publish/emergency deny policy with compromise cutoff; rotate credentials/root only as scope requires. Query registry, transparency/signing, provenance, promotion, admission, and runtime inventory for artifacts signed/published in window, including copies and manifests selected from indexes.

Quarantine suspicious digests, block new publish/promotion/admission, and tier running response by exploit evidence and service criticality: isolate/stop, drain, or closely monitor while replacing. Preserve artifacts/evidence/logs and investigate source, workflow, builder, dependencies, and signer use.

Rollback only to a digest passing current policy and unaffected trust; otherwise build forward from verified source on clean infrastructure. Reissue evidence, verify fleet convergence, revoke narrow exceptions/fallbacks, and measure cutoff-to-denial/remediation. Restore tests prove old signer and malicious digests cannot return.

### Answer 5

Inventory services, all image fields, registries/mirrors, tags/digests, build systems/runners, publish permissions, keys, CI/direct deployment, scanners/SBOM coverage, runtime inventory, and critical exceptions. Introduce digest resolution and observability first; warn on tags and persist resolved digest.

Create workload-identity release signing and platform provenance on ephemeral builds, then SBOM/scan attestations. Backtest existing production digests, classify missing/invalid evidence, repair pipelines, and issue narrow expiring legacy exceptions. Prevent new unsigned publish while grandfathered digests remain visible.

Canary admission by low-risk namespaces/services, verifying init/ephemeral/injected/multi-platform images and registry outage. Expand when latency, evidence freshness, remediation, and rollback meet targets. Finally require submitted digests, remove old keys/direct publish/tag-only/fail-open paths, expire exceptions, reconcile runtime, and test backup restore cannot resurrect them.
